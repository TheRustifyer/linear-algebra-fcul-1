\item Consider the matrices $A$ and $B$ from Exercise 5:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix}
    0 & 3 & 1 \\
    -1 & 0 & 2 \\
    3 & 0 & 2
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{bmatrix}
    -1 & 0 & 0 \\
    0 & -1 & 3 \\
    2 & 8 & 1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Determine all matrices $X$ satisfying

$$
2B^2 + \frac{1}{2}X - BA = (A-B)^2 + AB.
$$

\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{answer}
    Given $A, B \in \mathcal{M}_{3\times3}(\mathbb{R})$,
    we seek $X \in \mathcal{M}_{3\times3}(\mathbb{R})$.

    Expanding $(A-B)^2$ without assuming $AB=BA$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2B^2 + \frac{1}{2}X - BA
            &= (A-B)^2 + AB \\[0.5em]
            &= (A-B)(A-B) + AB \\[0.5em]
            &= A^2 - AB - BA + B^2 + AB \\[0.5em]
            &= A^2 - BA + B^2.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Adding $BA$ to both sides and isolating $X$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        2B^2 + \frac{1}{2}X
            &= A^2 + B^2 \\[0.5em]
        \frac{1}{2}X
            &= A^2 + B^2 - 2B^2 \\[0.5em]
            &= A^2 - B^2 \\[0.5em]
        X &= 2(A^2 - B^2).
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Computing $A^2$ and $B^2$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A^2 &= AA \\
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 3 & 1 \\
            -1 & 0 & 2 \\
            3 & 0 & 2
        \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 3 & 1 \\
            -1 & 0 & 2 \\
            3 & 0 & 2
        \end{bmatrix} \\[1em]
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 0 & 8 \\
            6 & -3 & 3 \\
            6 & 9 & 7
        \end{bmatrix}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        B^2 &= BB \\
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            -1 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & -1 & 3 \\
            2 & 8 & 1
        \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix}
            -1 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & -1 & 3 \\
            2 & 8 & 1
        \end{bmatrix} \\[1em]
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            1 & 0 & 0 \\
            6 & 25 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 25
        \end{bmatrix}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Substituting:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        X &= 2(A^2 - B^2) \\[0.5em]
          &= 2\left(
            \begin{bmatrix}
                0 & 0 & 8 \\
                6 & -3 & 3 \\
                6 & 9 & 7
            \end{bmatrix}
            -
            \begin{bmatrix}
                1 & 0 & 0 \\
                6 & 25 & 0 \\
                0 & 0 & 25
            \end{bmatrix}
          \right) \\[1em]
          &= 2\begin{bmatrix}
              -1 & 0 & 8 \\
              0 & -28 & 3 \\
              6 & 9 & -18
          \end{bmatrix} \\[1em]
          &= \boxed{
            \begin{bmatrix}
                -2 & 0 & 16 \\
                0 & -56 & 6 \\
                12 & 18 & -36
            \end{bmatrix}
          }.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Since the coefficient of $X$ is nonzero,
    this solution is unique.
\end{answer}
