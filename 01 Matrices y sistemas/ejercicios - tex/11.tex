\item Consider the matrix

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & 1 & 3 \\
    5 & 2 & 6 \\
    -2 & -1 & -3
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Verify that there exists a power of $A$ which is the zero matrix.\\

\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{answer}
    Given, $A\in\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbb{R})$ they are asking for: 
    $$
        \exists k\in\mathbb{N+}:
        A^k=0_3.
    $$

    Computing $A^2$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A^2 &= AA \\[0.5em]
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            1 & 1 & 3 \\
            5 & 2 & 6 \\
            -2 & -1 & -3
        \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix}
            1 & 1 & 3 \\
            5 & 2 & 6 \\
            -2 & -1 & -3
        \end{bmatrix} \\[1em]
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 0 & 0 \\
            3 & 3 & 9 \\
            -1 & -1 & -3
        \end{bmatrix}
        \neq 0_3.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Computing $A^3$:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
        A^3 &= A^2A \\[0.5em]
        &= \begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 0 & 0 \\
            3 & 3 & 9 \\
            -1 & -1 & -3
        \end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix}
            1 & 1 & 3 \\
            5 & 2 & 6 \\
            -2 & -1 & -3
        \end{bmatrix} \\[1em]
        &= \boxed{\begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 0
        \end{bmatrix}}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    Therefore,

    $$
    \boxed{
        \exists k=3\in\mathbb{N}^+:
        A^k=0_3
    }.
    $$
\end{answer}
