\setcounter{enumi}{46}
\item {\bfseries\boldmath
Without evaluating the determinants, show that
}

\[
\det\begin{bmatrix}a+b&b&3c\\x+y&y&3z\\3&2&9\end{bmatrix}=\det\begin{bmatrix}a+2x&x&3\\b+2y&y&6\\c+2z&z&9\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\begin{answer}
In the determinant on the left, factor $3$ out of column $3$ and then replace
column $1$ by column $1$ minus column $2$. Thus
\[
 \det\begin{bmatrix}a+b&b&3c\\x+y&y&3z\\3&2&9\end{bmatrix}
 =3\det\begin{bmatrix}a&b&c\\x&y&z\\1&2&3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
In the determinant on the right, replace column $1$ by column $1$ minus twice
column $2$, and factor $3$ out of column $3$:
\[
 \det\begin{bmatrix}a+2x&x&3\\b+2y&y&6\\c+2z&z&9\end{bmatrix}
 =3\det\begin{bmatrix}a&x&1\\b&y&2\\c&z&3\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The last two matrices are transposes of each other, so their determinants are
equal. This proves the required identity without evaluating them.
\end{answer}
